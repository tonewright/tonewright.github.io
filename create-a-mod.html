How to create a mod

Dump a character from the game, reshape her in Blender, and give her switches and textures of your own.

You start from the character as the game draws her, so there is nothing to extract or convert. An existing WWMI mod works the same way once Timbre has made it native (Add your mods): open it in Blender and skip step 1.

Words used here:

Word Means
dump A copy of a character as the game draws her, saved as a mod you can edit
part One section of the character the game draws, such as hair, body or jacket; Part <n> in Blender
piece One object of yours in Blender, inside a part
role What a texture does: diffuse for the colours, normalmap for the bumps, and so on
description overtone.mod.toml, the mod's settings file (Write a description)

#Before you start

  • Overtone is installed and the game starts with it (Getting started).
  • Blender is installed. Timbre finds it; if not, set it under Settings → Game → Blender.
  • The game shows each change a moment after you save it, while Reload on file change is on in the overlay's Settings, as it is unless you turned it off. F10 reloads every mod at once.

#1Dump the character

  1. In game, bring the character close to the camera. A dump keeps the detail the game is drawing, and from far away that is a simpler model.
  2. For a clean base, turn off every mod of that character, or press F8 to turn off all mods.
  3. Dump her one of three ways:
How Use it when
F11 Quickest. Dumps the character Overtone knows with the largest model on screen, usually the one you play.
F12 (Record) You will weight-paint, add pieces that bend with her, or need accurate bones. See below.
Overlay Tools → Picker You want a particular character, or one Overtone does not know: it asks Who is this? Press Dump or Record on her row.

Timbre's In game panel also has Dump.

A message says Dumping or Recording and her name. No known character on screen means Overtone recognised nobody: use the picker. Already capturing means a dump is still running. In the picker, her row reads Watching (or Recording), then Copying, then Dumped: and the folder, or Failed: and why. A dump takes a few seconds, longer while the game is still loading her textures.

#Record: better bones

Overtone works out the character's skeleton from how her bones move. Standing still, many bones never move, so their places are guesses. A recording watches her move for up to 3,600 frames, a minute at 60 FPS.

  1. Press F12, or Record in the picker.

  2. Move her: run, turn, jump, attack, crouch. A line at the top of the screen shows the time, the poses kept and how many bones have moved so far:

    REC 0:24    60 poses    151 of 180 bones moved

    no pose yet means none of her parts' bones has been read yet: keep her on screen.

  3. Stop when most bones have moved: press F12 again, or open the overlay and press Stop on that line. It also stops on its own at 3,600 frames, or when its memory budget is full.

Each later dump of the same character also improves her bones: Overtone pools up to eight earlier captures of her with the same parts.

#Where the dump goes

What Name
No mod on <Character> - Vanilla; her next costume <Character> (costume 2) - Vanilla
A form your mods name, such as her ultimate <Character> - <form> - Vanilla
A mod on <Character> - <mod> - Snapshot; several mods joined by +
Dumped too far away <Character> - Vanilla (lower detail) where Overtone can tell; dump her again closer
A character Overtone cannot name Unknown-<hex> - Vanilla

It lands in your library under character/<character>/, or character/others/ for a character Overtone cannot name. It starts off: double-click its tile in Timbre to turn it on. Untouched, it looks exactly like what was on screen.

A new dump of the same costume, form or mod replaces the last one, folder and all. These are kept:

  • A dump you opened in Blender or painted in Timbre's Paint: it moves aside as <Character> - Edited (or <Character> - <mod> - Edited).
  • A dump you renamed so its name no longer ends in - Vanilla or - Snapshot, or moved out of its character folder.

Changes to its description or textures alone do not keep it. Rename the folder before you edit it there.

Inside the folder:

File What it is
overtone.mod.toml The description: yours to edit
model.glb The geometry, opened in Blender. A character drawn as several meshes has a .glb for each.
Textures/ The game's textures, c<n>-<role>.dds, one per part and role the game's shader names
Textures/unnamed/ Textures the game bound that no role names: kept, used nowhere
.overtone/ Overtone's own files: never edit them

#2Edit it in Blender

  1. In Timbre, open the mod and press Blender. Each part is a Part <n> collection; the skeleton is Rig.
  2. Reshape, add or delete vertices, or weight-paint.
  3. Press Ctrl+S. Saving is exporting. The status bar says taken; the game draws it at its next reload, or not taken: and why, with a popup. The game shows it a moment later.
  • Add a piece: duplicate an object of the part it belongs to (Shift+D) and edit the copy. It keeps its part, its Armature modifier and its weights. The object's name is the piece's name.
  • A mesh from elsewhere: put it in the Part <n> collection whose shader it should wear, give it an Armature modifier on Rig, and weight it to the Rig's bones. A mesh with no Armature modifier is left out, and the status bar names it. A mesh with no weights is refused.
  • Hidden in Blender is hidden in game: a piece hidden by its eye, its monitor or its collection draws nothing. Hide or delete every object of a part and that part of the character is gone. A piece an option shows is the exception: the description decides it, whatever Blender shows. The sidebar's Overtone → State panel shows the pieces as each choice of an option draws them.
  • Renaming a piece an option names makes the next save refused until the description uses the new name too.
  • Blender without Timbre: use File → Import / Export → glTF on model.glb. On export, tick Data → Mesh → Tangents and Attributes, Data → Armature → Include All Bone Influences, and Include → Custom Properties. Saved without Tangents or Attributes, the file is refused and the message names them. Without Include All Bone Influences, weights past four per vertex are lost without a warning. A new object also needs the custom property overtone_part set to its part's number, which Timbre's Blender sets from its collection.

#3Make a piece switchable

  1. In Blender, name the object, for example Straw hat, and save.

  2. In Timbre, open the mod's Description and add:

    [option.Hat]
key     = "5"
default = "off"
on.show = ["Straw hat"]

  3. Press Save. In game, 5 puts the hat on and takes it off while the mod is on screen, and the overlay's Wardrobe lists the switch under the outfit.

A piece the description never mentions is always drawn. For more than on and off, list choices in the order the key steps through them, and give each its own show:

[option.Hair]
choices    = ["Short", "Long"]
default    = "Long"
key        = "/"
Short.show = ["Hair 0"]
Long.show  = ["Hair 1"]

#4Swap a texture

The dump's description places each texture by part and role:

[component.3]
diffuse = "Textures/c3-diffuse.dds"

3 is the part's number (Part 3 in Blender). Overwrite the file with your own, or point the role at a new file. A path starts at the folder of the part's mesh, which is the mod's folder in a dump; a leading / starts at the mod's.

  • Roles Overtone places: diffuse, lightmap, normalmap, materialmap and highlight.
  • A role can also be "game", to keep the game's texture, or a colour, "#rrggbb", which replaces the texture rather than tinting it.
  • Use DDS: it is drawn in its own format. A PNG or JPEG is drawn uncompressed, with no mipmaps.
  • A dumped texture you never changed still loads a second copy: delete its line, and the game's own is used.

To switch textures with a key, let a choice wear them:

[option.Colour]
choices = ["Black", "Blue"]
key     = "6"
Blue.wear.3 = { diffuse = "Textures/Jacket_Blue.dds" }

To texture one piece rather than a whole part, name a material, a set of roles, and give it to the piece:

[material.metal]
diffuse = "Textures/Metal_D.dds"

[piece.Buttons]
material = "metal"

Which texture wins. A piece with a material wears the material's textures, not its part's. While a choice that wears a part is chosen, what it wears replaces the part's for those roles. So a texture you change under [component.3] shows only where no material or wear covers it: if your change does not show, look for one.

Every key a description takes is in Write a description.

#5Check the result

Where What it tells you
Blender's status bar Whether each save was taken. Timbre also shows Last Blender save not applied: and why.
Timbre's Description Problems marked on their line as you type. A file that does not read is not saved, and Timbre says why.
overtone.log Each line of a description the game passed over, and why one did not read (Troubleshoot).
Timbre's Model The mod's geometry; its Variants set the switches to see what each changes.
The overlay's Wardrobe The mod's switches, under its outfit.

#Limits

  • A mod replaces parts the game draws; it cannot add a new part. A new piece is drawn with its part's shader.
  • Weights go only to the Rig's bones: a bone you add is refused. At most 256 bones per part.
  • A new shape key is refused. A face whose expression has not changed since it loaded is dumped without shape keys.
  • No new physics: weights on hair or skirt bones follow the game's own physics.
  • Glow maps and outline are not drawn yet.
  • Materials and textures painted in Blender change only the Blender preview.

Applies to Timbre 26.1019.0 and Overtone 26.1018.0.
