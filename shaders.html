Draw with your own shader

A mod's own HLSL shader in Overtone: turning on a mod's water or oil effect, and writing one, as a layer over a part or in place of the part's own shader.

How-to9 min readTimbre 26.1123.0 · Overtone 26.1123.0

Some mods bring a shader of their own: water running over skin, an oil sheen, a glint. Overtone draws it from the mod's HLSL file:

  • A WWMI mod's effect comes across. Timbre reads its ini, and the key or switch that turned the effect on still does.
  • Your HLSL as you wrote it for 3DMigoto. The same main, the same IniParams, the same registers.
  • No hashes and no shader overrides. A few lines of the mod's description say which part wears the shader.
  • The game never compiles. Timbre does, as it opens, and keeps the result for the game to read.

#Before you start

  • Timbre on Windows. A Mac or Linux Timbre compiles no shader.
  • Settings → Play → Add-ons → Shader compiler says Ready. If it says Missing, press Download.
  • The mod is in your library and draws in game (Add your mods).
  • To write a shader: you can edit the mod's description (Write a description), and you know HLSL.
Word Means
description overtone.mod.toml, the mod's settings file
part One section of the character the game draws, such as her body: Part 3 in Timbre's Model
piece One object of a part, by its name in Blender
material A named set of textures and, here, a shader, which parts and pieces wear
layer A material drawn again over a part or a piece, after it: how water lies on skin

#Turn on a mod's effect

An effect a WWMI mod turned on with a key is a switch in Overtone, named as its ini named it. Start the game with mods (Play with mods), then turn the switch on either way:

Way Do
Its key Press the key the mod's author gave it
The overlay Press Home, open the mod's row in Wardrobe, turn the switch on

The effect shows on the character a moment later.

#When an effect does not show

The log names the shader and why it did not draw (Where to look). Search it for the shader's file name.

What you see Why What to do
The log says is not compiled yet Timbre has not opened since the mod came in, or its shader compiler is missing Check Shader compiler says Ready, close the game, close and open Timbre, start the game
The log says did not compile: and an error The HLSL has a fault the compiler names Tell the mod's author, with the error
In Timbre's Model, the piece says Not drawn here: its shader is made for another of the game's shaders It reads a buffer the game's shader for that part does not hold Tell the mod's author
A layer you wrote does not draw, and Inside marks it Its on names no part or piece, or one found twice in the mod Name one piece by its part: "3/Skin"
The effect covers the whole body, where it should cover a patch A layer covers all of its part or piece Nothing yet: see What you can't do yet
The game's own shader draws, changed The mod patched the game's shader ([ShaderRegex]), which Overtone never does Nothing: that part of the mod is left out

#Your first shader

This lays a red tint over one part. It takes about a minute.

  1. In Timbre, open the mod's Model and note a part's number, such as Part 3.

  2. In the mod's folder, make Shaders/Tint.hlsl:

    float4 main(float4 pos : SV_Position) : SV_Target0
{
    return float4(0.25, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0);
}

  3. Open the mod's Inside and click a row. Its description opens beside it.

  4. Add this at the end, with your part's number alone:

    [material.tint]
over  = "own"
hlsl  = { pixel = "Shaders/Tint.hlsl" }
blend = "add"
depth = "equal"
write = "rgb"

[layer.Tint]
on       = "3"
material = "tint"

    If the file has a heading for that part by name, such as [part.skirt], write that name, "skirt".

  5. Press Save (Ctrl+S).

  6. Close the game if it runs, then close and open Timbre. Timbre compiles the shader as it opens.

  7. Start the game. The part is redder.

Timbre compiles a mod's shaders only as it opens, and only while the game is closed, so repeat steps 6 and 7 after each edit of an .hlsl file.

#How Overtone runs your shader

  • It replaces the stages you ship, in the colour pass alone. Shadows, depth and the outline keep the game's shader.

  • A stage you leave out is the part's own. A pixel shader alone reads what the game's vertex shader writes, by semantic. In the game, a character's first UV is TEXCOORD0 and its normal, in the world's axes, TEXCOORD11. Where your pixel shader reads an input the vertex shader does not write, the part draws in its own shader and a layer is left out.

  • It compiles as 3DMigoto compiles it. Entry main, VERTEX_SHADER or PIXEL_SHADER defined as 1, read as fxc reads HLSL (no short-circuit &&, || and ?:, and a vector condition taken per component), to shader model 6.0. hlsl = "Shaders/Water.hlsl" compiles one file as both stages, so it holds both under #if VERTEX_SHADER and #if PIXEL_SHADER. A pixel shader alone is named as pixel in a table, as in your first shader.

  • A register reads the first of these that has it:

    The register Reads
    One the material's inputs names That file, game texture or nothing
    t120 The parameters, as IniParams (Read a switch, a number or the clock)
    In the pixel stage, one the part's textures sit at That texture: its colour, normal map, light map
    One the game's shader for that stage also reads The game's, as it bound it
    Any other Zeros

    A constant buffer your inputs leave alone is the game's at that register. A sampler is the game's where its shader declares one, else s0 to s15 in DirectX 11's default state.

#Lay an effect over a part

A layer draws its material over a part or a piece after the part is drawn. Its material says how it mixes in:

Key Value For an effect over the part
over "own" The textures are those of what wears it
blend "add", "alpha", "multiply" Add light (a glint), cover by your alpha, or darken
depth "equal" Draws only on the part's own surface, never in front of it
write "rgb" Writes colour only, of the first target; the rest of the pass stays the game's

A layer draws while its when holds, as 'Oil' for an on-off switch called Oil, and always without one. Its opacity, 0 to 1, counts only where its material has no blend. Two layers over two pieces are two tables: [layer.water] and [layer."water.2"].

#Read the part's own textures

A part's textures sit at the registers the game's shader reads them at, which differ from part to part. To read one at a register of your choice, name its role:

[material.wet]
over          = "own"
inputs.ps-t12 = { worn = "diffuse" }

t12 then holds the colour texture the part wears now: the mod's, your Paint, or the game's where neither gives one. Without over, it is the material's own diffuse, else the game's. It works at a pixel texture below ps-t64, by a role of Roles.

#Read a texture of your own

Name the file at the register your shader declares it at:

[material.wet]
inputs.ps-t10 = "Textures/WetMask.dds"
Texture2D<float4> WetMask : register(t10);

A file starts at the mod's folder; .. reaches beside it, within the mod's pack (Ship your shaders). A cube map is read as a cube, and a buffer by its format (Materials). false binds nothing there.

#Read a switch, a number or the clock

params sets the lanes x0 to w255 your shader reads from t120:

[material.wet]
params = { x200 = "Wet", y200 = 0.5, x255 = { time = 1 } }
Texture1D<float4> IniParams : register(t120);
float wet = IniParams[200].x;   // the Wet switch: 0 off, 1 on
float t   = IniParams[255].x;   // the clock in seconds, times 1

A lane takes a number, the name of a switch, { time = 1 } for the clock times that number, or a list of rows, each a when and a value, the first that holds setting it. A switch gives its choice: 1 on and 0 off, a numbered choice its number, a named one its WWMI value, or its place from 0 in a switch you wrote. A slider cannot be named. A lane you leave out reads 0.

#Read the game's textures and buffers

[material.wet]
inputs.ps-t13  = { game = "matcap", part = 3 }   # the game's matcap of part 3
inputs.ps-cb13 = { game = "ps-cb4" }             # the game's pixel buffer 4, read at b13

game at a texture takes a role of Roles, and part a part by its number or its name. The game's texture binds once play has kept it on this machine, and only where the mod names its object; until then your shader is passed over. A game buffer at the register the game binds it at needs no line.

A game buffer you read must be one the game's shader for that part declares, read no further than the 256-byte block its declaration ends in. Otherwise the part draws in its own shader, and its piece in Model says In the part's own shader: its shader is made for another of the game's shaders.

#Replace a part's own shader

Give the part or piece the material, rather than a layer:

[material.glass]
over = "own"
hlsl = { pixel = "Shaders/Glass.hlsl" }

[piece.Visor]
material = "glass"

Where your shader cannot draw, the part's own draws it. Add only to the hlsl table, and it is drawn by your shader or not at all, and casts no shadow:

hlsl = { pixel = "Shaders/Glass.hlsl", only = true }

#Ship your shaders

  • Every file in the pack. A mod's pack is the folder it lies nested in with its other versions, else its own folder. A shader and the files it #includes are read from inside it; a path out of it is refused.
  • Sizes. Each file up to 4 MiB, a shader and its includes up to 16 MiB and 64 files.
  • Kept. Timbre keeps each compiled stage on this machine and compiles it again once its file changes.
  • DXBC alone. A shader shipped only as fxc's compiled file, or 3DMigoto's cache beside HLSL that does not compile, is converted and drawn.
  • Room. A material reads at most 16 inputs and one game buffer by game. A vertex shader reads only what the game's vertex layout for the part carries, and one that leaves the shape keys unread draws in the part's own shader.

#What you can't do yet

  • Cover part of a piece. A layer covers all of its part or piece.
  • Patch the game's own shader. A [ShaderRegex] section is left out.
  • Draw in another pass. Your shader draws in the colour pass alone.
  • Blend another way. A WWMI layer blending any way but alpha, add, multiply, premultiplied or a fixed strength is left out.
  • Move a parameter by a key that steps it. A variable no switch offers is sent at its first value.
  • Draw it on a Mac or Linux.
