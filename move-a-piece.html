Move a piece to another character

Move a skirt from one character's mod to another character in Blender, start to finish, and see it swing in game.

Tutorial3 min readTimbre 26.1106.0 · Overtone 26.1106.0

In about ten minutes the skirt of one mod is on another character, in game, swinging with her walk. You rename no vertex groups: Overtone moves the skirt's weights onto her skeleton as you save. A skirt is the example here. A top or a hat goes the same way.

#Before you start

  • Blender is set up (Before you start).
  • The wearer, the character who gets the skirt, is a mod of yours: a dump of her, or any mod you edit (Dump the character). Its bones must include a skirt of her own to swing on.
  • The donor, the mod the skirt comes from, is native, and the skirt is a piece of its own there: a mesh apart from the legs.
  • The game is running with Overtone and the wearer's mod is on (See your changes in game).

#1Make the donor's model

  1. In Timbre, press Blender on the donor, and wait for it to open.
  2. Close Blender.
  3. In Timbre, press Folder on the donor.

The donor's folder is open, and it holds a .glb file, the model you take the skirt from.

#2Open the wearer

  1. In Timbre, press Blender on the wearer (only Blender from Timbre takes a mesh in).

You see her Part collections and Rig.

#3Bring in the donor

  1. Choose File → Import → glTF 2.0 (.glb/.gltf).
  2. Pick the donor's .glb file.

The donor's pieces and its skeleton appear on top of the wearer, outside every Part collection.

#4Keep only the skirt

  1. Click one of the donor's pieces in the list at the top right. It lights up in the view.
  2. If it is not the skirt, press X in the view and confirm.
  3. Repeat until only the skirt's pieces and the donor's skeleton are left.

The wearer stands in the view with the skirt on her. Keep the skeleton for now: Overtone reads it to place the weights.

#5Put the skirt on a part

  1. Select every piece of the skirt.
  2. Press M and pick a part of her clothes, such as Lower body · Part 4.

The skirt's pieces now sit inside that Part collection.

#6Save

  1. Press Ctrl+S.

The status bar says what it took, such as Skirt taken into Part 4: 9 groups by word, 27 groups by chain, 2 groups to the nearest bone. by chain means a chain, a line of bones that swings, went onto a chain of hers. The game shows the skirt a moment later, in her part's textures, so it looks patchy for now.

If the status bar says not taken in:, not taken: or was not written:, read the reason in When a save is not taken.

#7See it swing

  1. In the game, walk and run.

The skirt moves with her legs and swings on her own skirt bones. It keeps the donor's shape, so it can dip into her body in places. Longer than her own, it swings only so far down, and below that it follows her thighs. Half as long again and more, it swings on bones of its own (What keeps the weights it came with).

#8Delete the donor's skeleton

  1. Click the skeleton in the list that is not Rig.
  2. Press X in the view and confirm.

Only Rig is left, and the skirt still moves with it.

#9Bring its textures

  1. In Timbre, open the donor's Inside and click a row: its description names the skirt's textures.
  2. Copy those files from the donor's folder into the wearer's.
  3. Follow Give a piece its own textures.

The skirt wears its own look in game.

#Next

  • Skin showing through, or the skirt cutting into her? Move the vertices in Edit Mode and save, and hide what the skirt covers (Hide or remove pieces).
  • A chain swings wrongly? Paint weights in Blender, and see What keeps the weights it came with for what a save reads.
  • The Wuthering Waves skeleton: which bones hold the skin, and what catches authors out.

Applies to Timbre 26.1106.0 and Overtone 26.1106.0.
